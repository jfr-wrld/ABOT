# Route BLACK PRO through Play and QRIS

## Ringkasan

ABOT sebaiknya menjual BLACK PRO lewat dua jalur. Di aplikasi Android, gunakan **Google Play Billing**: langganan bulanan Rp 29.000 dan tahunan Rp 199.000 sebagai dua *base plan* dalam satu produk langganan, dan lifetime sebagai produk sekali beli. Di web/PWA, gunakan **checkout sekali bayar lewat Midtrans** (QRIS, e-wallet, VA), satu-satunya gateway besar yang terkonfirmasi menerima pemilik perorangan dengan KTP saja. Di web, jual paket tahunan, lifetime dan Founders, tanpa perpanjangan otomatis dulu. User Choice Billing (UCB) dan merchant-of-record (Lemon Squeezy, Paddle) belum layak untuk tahap awal. Untuk jumlah transaksi yang sama, penjualan lewat QRIS di web menghasilkan sekitar **30% lebih banyak** daripada Play (Play memotong 15% dan harga Play sudah termasuk PPN 11%). Namun aplikasi Android **tidak boleh** mengarahkan pengguna ke harga web. Secara pajak, penjual perorangan dengan omzet di bawah Rp 4,8 miliar tidak wajib PKP sehingga tidak memungut PPN. PPh final 0,5% berlaku tanpa batas waktu (PP 20/2026), dan Rp 500 juta pertama setahun bebas pajak. Pemilik tetap perlu NPWP, NIB, pendaftaran PSE di Komdigi, dan kebijakan refund tertulis. Untuk arsitektur, jadikan **satu tabel `entitlements` di Supabase** sebagai sumber kebenaran. Tabel ini diisi webhook RevenueCat (sisi Play; gratis di bawah US$2.500 MTR) dan webhook Midtrans. Perangkat menyimpan **token bertanda tangan Ed25519** dengan `valid_until`, sehingga PRO tetap jalan offline. Harga tetap seperti draf. Pilih tahunan sebagai default, beri trial 7 hari di paket tahunan, batasi kursi Founders Rp 399.000, dan biarkan pencatatan latihan, riwayat penuh dan ekspor data tetap gratis. Semua ini membutuhkan **pengecualian tertulis dari pemilik** atas aturan "Zero External Paid API: no runtime network calls, no tokens" di CLAUDE.md. Banyak angka biaya berasal dari cuplikan hasil pencarian karena situs vendor diblokir, jadi daftar verifikasi di bagian akhir wajib dicek sebelum peluncuran.

ABOT's payment spec should be **Google Play Billing for everything sold inside the Android app, plus a one-off Midtrans QRIS/e-wallet checkout on the Vercel PWA, both writing into one Supabase `entitlements` table that the device caches as a signed, time-bounded token**. Play is non-negotiable on Android unless ABOT enrols in User Choice Billing, and in Indonesia UCB saves only 4 percentage points while adding a second payment provider to run. The web channel matters because it keeps roughly 30% more of each rupiah: an individual seller below the PKP threshold charges no PPN, while Play prices carry 11% effective VAT and a 15% fee. RevenueCat should handle the Play side, because it is free below US$2,500 monthly tracked revenue and removes the three-day acknowledgement risk that a pausing free-tier Supabase project would create. The draft prices (Rp 29.000, Rp 199.000, Rp 798.000, founders Rp 399.000) hold up against Hevy, Strong, Spotify and Apple Fitness+ anchors, provided annual is the default, the founders tier is capped, and the free tier keeps logging, history and export. None of this fits the current CLAUDE.md rule against runtime network calls, so the owner must approve a scoped exception before any code is written. Several vendor sites were blocked during research, so fee tables, RevenueCat's Capacitor 8 support and Play's payment-policy wording on redeem codes all need a direct check before launch.

## Play Billing inside the app, QRIS on the web, nothing that steers between them

On Android the decision is mostly made by Google. Digital subscriptions and lifetime unlocks sold inside a Play app must use Play Billing unless the developer enrols in User Choice Billing, and Google's UCB page lists Indonesia with a fee "reduced by 4% for transactions where users pay with an alternative billing system" ([Play Console Help](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/13821247?hl=en), read from a search excerpt). UCB does not remove Play Billing: the app must offer both, and the user picks ([Play Console Help](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/13821247?hl=en)). Indonesia's competition case raises the ceiling but has not yet changed the floor. KPPU fined Google **Rp 202,5 miliar** in January 2025 and ordered it to open UCB to all developers with a discount of at least 5% ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/01/22/google-hit-with-12-6m-fine-in-indonesia-for-monopolistic-practices-in-payment-system)), and the Supreme Court rejected Google's appeal in March 2026 ([Xinhua](https://english.news.cn/20260313/ae94e8ba64944f0f872c1051cc3b0c96/c.html); [CNBC Indonesia](https://www.cnbcindonesia.com/tech/20260317140204-37-719813/kasasi-ditolak-google-bayar-denda-rp-2025-miliar-di-indonesia/amp)). No Google page found in this research confirms that the 5% figure is in effect. Google's broader billing-choice programme with web link-outs started on 30 June 2026 in the US, UK and EEA ([gHacks](https://www.ghacks.net/2026/06/25/google-play-billing-choice-program-goes-live-june-30-with-lower-developer-fees-and-third-party-billing/)), and one Indonesian outlet reports it reaches Indonesia on **30 September 2027** ([Journalarta](https://journalarta.com/en/technology/impact-google-play-store-changes-indonesian-game-developers/)), which is secondary and unconfirmed by Google.

UCB is the wrong first step for a one-person app. Saving four points on Android sales would mean integrating Midtrans inside the Android app through Google's alternative billing APIs, handling support for two billing paths, and still paying Google about 11%. Play Billing also solves the problem that matters most in Indonesia: payment methods. Google lists **DANA, DOKU, GoPay, OVO and ShopeePay** for Indonesian Play users ([Google Play Help](https://support.google.com/googleplay/answer/2651410?hl=en&co=GENIE.CountryCode%3DID)), plus carrier billing on Telkomsel, Indosat, XL/AXIS, Tri and Smartfren, though Google warns that some carriers cannot pay for subscriptions and prepaid numbers may not be supported ([Google Play Help](https://support.google.com/googleplay/answer/2651410?hl=en&co=GENIE.CountryCode%3DID)). Credit cards are only **5% of Indonesian e-commerce value**, against 42% for wallets and 34% for account-to-account transfers ([Gazet International, citing Worldpay GPR 2026](https://gazetinternational.com/digital-wallets-overtake-cash-and-cards-in-indonesias-e-commerce-market/)), so any channel that is effectively card-only fails the core market.

That fact rules out merchant-of-record services for Indonesian buyers. Lemon Squeezy charges **5% + US$0.50** per sale before payout deductions ([Lemon Squeezy docs](https://docs.lemonsqueezy.com/help/getting-started/fees)), and no source confirmed that any MoR offers QRIS or Indonesian e-wallets at checkout. Their main value, collecting and remitting VAT across countries, does not apply to domestic sales by a non-PKP Indonesian seller who owes no PPN in the first place. Stripe's Indonesian local-method support is contradicted between two low-quality sources ([LearnWithHasan](https://learnwithhasan.com/payment-gateways/country/indonesia/)), so treat it as unknown. An MoR earns a place only later, for international buyers paying the USD prices already present in ABOT's remote config.

For the web, Midtrans is the practical choice because it is the only large gateway confirmed to onboard an **individual with a KTP** ([Midtrans Passport](https://midtrans.com/passport)), while Xendit states it does not accept individual businesses ([Xendit Help](https://help.xendit.co/hc/en-us/articles/360035083911-Can-Individual-businesses-use-Xendit-s-services)) and would need at least a registered sole proprietorship. True auto-renewal in Indonesia works only on cards or GoPay tokenization, and Midtrans enables PIN-less recurring GoPay only after activation through its sales team ([Midtrans GoPay Tokenization](https://docs.midtrans.com/reference/direct-debit-api-gopay-tokenization)). QRIS and virtual accounts are one-off by nature. The web channel should therefore sell **one-off products**: annual, lifetime, founders, and optionally a non-renewing 30-day pass. Mayar is a credible fallback with a public payment-link API and an MoR-style fee model ([Mayar docs](https://docs.mayar.id/mor/fees.md)), but its per-method rates sat in an unreadable image and webhook support is unconfirmed. Creator storefronts such as Lynk.id (3% reported, [Dorangadget](https://dorangadget.com/?p=122220)) or Karyakarsa (10%, [Karyakarsa Help](https://help.karyakarsa.com/karyakarsa-usage-guide/creator-guides/kebijakan-mengenai-gajiandikaryakarsa)) can work as a zero-backend stopgap that delivers pre-generated redeem codes, if the platform hands each buyer a unique item, which was not verified.

The two channels must stay separate. Outside the US and EEA programmes, linking from the Android app to a cheaper web checkout is outside Play policy unless the developer is enrolled in a qualifying programme ([Neon](https://www.neonpay.com/blog/google-plays-new-u.s.-billing-linking-policies-what-game-developers-need-to-know); [Play Console Help 16505463](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/16505463?hl=en)). The Play build should show only Play prices and Play purchase buttons, and never mention the web price. A user who bought on the web and signs in on Android receiving PRO through their account is the common multi-platform pattern, and typing a redeem code into the app is commonly tolerated, but the current Payments policy text was not readable from this session, so the redeem-code screen in the Play build is a **verify-before-launch item**. The PWA itself sits entirely outside Play policy.

## A QRIS sale keeps about 30% more than the same sale on Play

Play's fee for markets not yet on Google's new structure is **15% on the first US$1M of yearly revenue** and 15% on auto-renewing subscriptions from day one ([Play Console Help](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/112622?hl=en), excerpt), so all three ABOT products, lifetime included, fall in the 15% bracket. Google determines, collects and remits Indonesian VAT on Play sales ([Google notice, Jul 2020](https://mail.kde.org/pipermail/marble-devel/2020-July/009607.html)), and after PMK 131/2024 the effective rate is **11%** (12% on an 11/12 base), which Play returned to ([Bloomberg Technoz](https://www.bloombergtechnoz.com/detail-news/59539/toko-aplikasi-google-balik-ke-tarif-ppn-11-ini-kata-perusahaan/2)). The IDR price a user sees on Play therefore includes VAT, and the developer receives the VAT-exclusive price minus 15%.

On the web, the picture inverts. QRIS merchant discount is set by Bank Indonesia at a regular **0.7%**, borne by the merchant and not passable to the buyer ([Bank Indonesia](https://www.bi.go.id/id/publikasi/ruang-media/cerita-bi/Pages/mdr-QRIS.aspx)). Third-party summaries of Midtrans pricing give QRIS 0.70% (VAT-inclusive), GoPay and ShopeePay 2.00%, OVO and DANA 1.50%, VA Rp 4.000 flat and cards 2.9% + Rp 2.000, with non-QRIS fees quoted before 11% VAT ([CheckoutFlow](https://checkoutflow.pages.dev/id-en/best-payment-gateway-ecommerce/midtrans-pricing/)). Another source gives the card fee as 2.9% + Rp 2.500 ([Dodo Payments](https://dodopayments.com/zh/blogs/midtrans-alternatives)). None of these came from Midtrans's own pricing page, which was blocked.

The table below estimates what ABOT receives per sale. Play figures assume 15% fee on the VAT-exclusive price. Midtrans figures add 11% VAT to the non-QRIS fees and exclude withdrawal costs. The MoR row uses an assumed Rp 16.500 per US$, which is not from a cited source. The UCB row assumes Google's 11% fee applies to the full price and that no PPN is due on the alternative path, both unverified.

| Product (price) | Play Billing | Midtrans QRIS | Midtrans GoPay | Midtrans VA | UCB + QRIS (est.) | Lemon Squeezy (card) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Monthly (Rp 29.000) | Rp 22.207 (77%) | Rp 28.797 (99%) | Rp 28.356 | Rp 24.560 | Rp 25.607 | Rp 19.300 (67%) |
| Annual (Rp 199.000) | Rp 152.387 | Rp 197.607 | Rp 194.582 | Rp 194.560 | Rp 175.717 | Rp 180.800 |
| Founders lifetime (Rp 399.000) | Rp 305.541 | Rp 396.207 | Rp 390.142 | Rp 394.560 | Rp 352.317 | Rp 370.800 |
| Lifetime (Rp 798.000) | Rp 611.081 | Rp 792.414 | Rp 780.284 | Rp 793.560 | Rp 704.634 | Rp 749.850 |

Three conclusions follow. First, a QRIS sale nets about **Rp 1,30 for every Rp 1 a Play sale nets**, at every price point, because the web seller pays neither the 15% fee nor the 11% VAT. That gap is large enough to justify building the web checkout early, especially for the high-value lifetime and founders products. Second, the flat **Rp 4.000 VA fee eats about 15% of a monthly sale** but under 1% of a lifetime sale, which is another reason the web should not sell monthly at launch. Third, a fixed per-transaction fee makes MoRs punishing at Rp 29.000 and acceptable only at lifetime prices. UCB lands between Play and direct web, and since it adds the most engineering for the smallest gain on Android, it waits.

Tooling costs are near zero at launch scale. RevenueCat is reported free up to **US$2,500 MTR**, then 1% ([pricepush](https://pricepush.app/glossary/monthly-tracked-revenue); [costbench](https://costbench.com/software/subscription-billing/revenuecat/free-plan/)); one aggregator says there is no free tier, so this needs confirming on RevenueCat's own page. At the assumed exchange rate, US$2,500 is about Rp 41 juta of monthly revenue, roughly 1,400 monthly subscribers. Supabase's free plan is reported at 500K Edge Function invocations a month, but free projects pause after seven days of inactivity, and Pro costs US$25 a month ([designrevision](https://designrevision.com/blog/supabase-pricing.md)). Lifetime revenue also arrives up front, which helps early cash flow but borrows from future renewals, a trade-off the pricing section addresses.

## An individual seller under Rp 4,8 miliar charges no PPN but still has paperwork

The tax position for an individual owner is favourable. A seller with turnover at or below **Rp 4,8 miliar a year** is a *pengusaha kecil*, is not required to register as PKP, and therefore does not charge PPN, though voluntary registration is allowed ([DDTC on PMK 81/2024](https://news.ddtc.co.id/berita/nasional/1806903/pmk-81-revisi-ketentuan-tempat-pelaporan-usaha-pkp-begini-detailnya); [IKPI](https://ikpi.or.id/pengusaha-wajib-daftar-pkp-kecuali-kategori-ini/)). Web prices should be shown as final, with no PPN line. Income tax is lighter still. **PP 20/2026**, enacted 22 April 2026, keeps the 0.5% final UMKM rate and removes the time limit for individuals and PT Perorangan ([DDTC](https://news.ddtc.co.id/berita/nasional/1819741/akhirnya-aturan-baru-soal-pph-final-umkm-05-persen-resmi-terbit); [DDTC](https://news.ddtc.co.id/berita/nasional/1819737/pp-direvisi-pph-final-umkm-cuma-untuk-op-pt-perorangan-dan-koperasi)), and individuals pay nothing on the first **Rp 500 juta** of annual turnover ([DDTC](https://news.ddtc.co.id/literasi/kamus/1811984/apa-itu-pph-final-umkm)). An older Mekari post saying individual use of the 0.5% rate ended in January 2026 is superseded. Rp 500 juta is about 2.500 annual plans at list price, so ABOT will likely owe no income tax in its first year. Whether Play income counts toward turnover at the gross price or the net payout was not established; ask a tax consultant.

The administrative obligations are the real cost. The owner needs an NPWP (now NIK-based through Coretax, [OCBC](https://www.ocbc.id/id/article/2026/01/06/coretax-daftar-npwp-cara-registrasi-npwp-online-terbaru-lewat-sistem-coretax)) and an NIB through OSS as a perorangan business. Private electronic system operators must register as **PSE** under PM Kominfo 5/2020, with sanctions up to access blocking ([Katadata](https://katadata.co.id/digital/e-commerce/6ab0ed9861fca/komdigi-25-pse-lingkup-privat-belum-lapor-ada-whoosh-hingga-lion-air)). Enforcement is live: Komdigi warned 25 unregistered PSEs in July 2026, **including the fitness app Strava** ([Bisnis](https://teknologi.bisnis.com/read/20260702/101/1985029/komdigi-catat-25-perusahaan-belum-daftar-pse-qatar-airways-hingga-strava)), and set a 1 October 2026 deadline for a later batch ([Bisnis](https://teknologi.bisnis.com/read/20260928/101/2007757/komdigi-beri-tenggat-1-oktober-7-pse-belum-terdaftar-terancam-kena-teguran)). Whether a small individual app is expected to register in practice is unclear, but registration through OSS is cheap insurance once ABOT takes money. On consumer protection, PP 80/2019 requires e-commerce sellers to guarantee consumer rights, including cancellation and exchange, and lets consumers complain to the Minister ([Setkab](https://setkab.go.id/?p=176339); [PP 80/2019 text](https://peraturan.go.id/files/pp80-2019bt.pdf)). No statutory cooling-off period for digital subscriptions was found, so a clear written refund and cancellation policy on the checkout page is the defensible minimum.

The Play developer account type creates a timing constraint. Personal accounts created after 13 November 2023 must run a **closed test with at least 12 testers for 14 continuous days** before applying for production, while organisation accounts with a D-U-N-S number are exempt ([Play Console Help](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/14151465?hl=en), excerpt; [Choicely](https://www.choicely.com/blog/google-play-12-tester-rule)). Every app update has also needed **Play Billing Library 8 or later** since 31 August 2026, with extensions ending 1 November 2026 ([RevenueCat](https://www.revenuecat.com/blog/engineering/play-billing-8-migration/)), so as of today any billing integration must start on PBL 8 or 9. A PT Perorangan keeps the 0.5% income-tax scheme and may ease a D-U-N-S application, which is a reason to weigh it against staying a plain individual.

## One Supabase table decides who is PRO, and the phone carries a signed copy

The existing design is a sound start with three weaknesses. `user_entitlements` holds one row per user with a single `tier` and `pro_expires_at`, written only by security-definer functions, and `abot_apply_grant` already merges grants sensibly, with lifetime beating pro and extra days stacking (`supabase/migrations/20261109_admin_and_redeem.sql`). The first weakness is that one row cannot tell sources apart: if a Play subscription is refunded, nothing records which days came from Play and which from a VIP code. The second is that the client never moves backwards. `mergeAccountEntitlement` in `src/lib/entitlement-sync.ts` lets "pro time only move forward", so a refund or chargeback can never reach a device. The third is that the device's tier is a plain field in the persisted Zustand store, and the built-in offline codes in `src/lib/redeem-service.ts` are SHA-256 hashes in a public repository, which the code itself already treats as public promo codes that never grant lifetime.

The target model keeps the same functions and adds a ledger. An append-only `purchase_events` table, unique on `(provider, provider_event_id)`, absorbs webhook retries idempotently; a `purchases` table records each Play token or gateway order with its state; and an `entitlements` table holds one row per user per source (`play`, `web`, `code`, `legacy`, `admin`) with `expires_at` and `revoked_at`. The effective tier is derived by a `get_my_entitlement()` RPC that also mints the device token. Google's own guidance supports this shape: de-duplicate RTDN by `messageId` and always re-read state from the Developer API rather than trusting the notification ([Android RTDN reference](https://developer.android.com/google/play/billing/rtdn-reference)), and expect order ids to change per renewal and tokens to change on upgrade via `linkedPurchaseToken` ([Android subscription lifecycle](https://developer.android.com/google/play/billing/lifecycle/subscriptions)). Migration is mechanical: copy `user_entitlements` rows into `entitlements` with `source='legacy'`, turn `user_entitlements` into a compatibility view so `entitlement-sync.ts` keeps working during the transition, and have `redeem_code` and `admin_grant` insert `source='code'` or `'admin'` rows plus a `purchase_events` entry. Founder unit codes stay on the user record and are unaffected.

### RevenueCat for Play, an Edge Function for the web

The Play side should run on RevenueCat rather than a self-hosted verifier. Google requires purchases to be acknowledged **within three days** or they are "automatically refunded and entitlement revoked", and it cannot tell a deliberate rejection from a backend timeout ([Android: Integrate](https://developer.android.com/google/play/billing/integrate)). A self-hosted design needs a Google service account minting RS256 tokens in Deno, a `verify-purchase` function, a Pub/Sub push endpoint with OIDC verification ([Google Cloud](https://docs.cloud.google.com/pubsub/docs/authenticate-push-subscriptions)), voided-purchase handling and a reconciler. That is several days of work, and on Supabase's free tier the seven-day inactivity pause would silently stop acknowledgements and refund real buyers. RevenueCat absorbs all of this at no cost below its threshold. Its alternatives are weaker fits: Glassfy shut down around the end of 2024, and Adapty, Qonversion and Apphud free tiers (US$5K-10K) are reported inconsistently ([sph.sh](https://sph.sh/en/posts/revenuecat-alternatives-comparison-2026/)).

Identity must be the Supabase user id from the first launch. RevenueCat's `logIn` after a purchase leaves the purchase on an anonymous id, webhooks then carry that anonymous id, and aliasing does not fire a TRANSFER event ([RevenueCat community #5562](https://community.revenuecat.com/sdks-51/issue-with-anonymous-app-user-id-on-webhook-breaking-subscription-flow-5562); [#6767](https://community.revenuecat.com/general-questions-7/no-transfer-webhook-when-logging-in-after-anonymous-purchase-6767)). Supabase anonymous sign-in gives a stable UUID that survives upgrading to a Google account, per a developer report ([FlutterFlow community](https://community.flutterflow.io/database-and-apis/post/revenuecat-and-supabase-auth-PODpamB2b3k7ldI)). ABOT should therefore require at least anonymous Supabase auth before the paywall, configure RevenueCat with `auth.uid()`, and point RevenueCat webhooks at a Supabase Edge Function that writes `entitlements` rows with `source='play'`, returning 200 even for unmapped users so RevenueCat does not retry forever ([community #6557](https://community.revenuecat.com/third-party-integrations-53/error-extracting-app-user-id-from-webhook-in-supabase-400-user-id-not-found-6557)). The web side needs no RevenueCat at all: a `create-checkout` function creates a Midtrans Snap order tied to the uid, and a `payment-webhook` function verifies the signature, re-queries transaction status, and upserts the entitlement. If the owner wants RevenueCat to show web purchases in its dashboard, the v1 promotional-grant endpoint can mirror them ([RevenueCat API v1](https://www.revenuecat.com/docs/api-v1/entitlements)), but whether it accepts a `lifetime` duration was not confirmed.

Two compatibility gaps need checking first. No RevenueCat statement of **Capacitor 8** support was found; `@revenuecat/purchases-capacitor` 10.x and later require Capacitor 7+ ([newreleases.io](https://newreleases.io/project/github/RevenueCat/purchases-capacitor/release/10.0.0)). Capawesome's purchases plugin explicitly lists Capacitor 8 ([Capawesome](https://capawesome.io/plugins/purchases)), and Fovea's `capacitor-plugin-cdv-purchase` builds against PBL 9 with native Capacitor bridges ([Fovea release notes](https://github.com/j3k0/cordova-plugin-purchase/blob/master/RELEASE_NOTES.md)), though a recent regression there affected non-consumable ownership events, which matters for the lifetime SKU. If RevenueCat's plugin fails on Capacitor 8, the fallback is one of those plugins with RevenueCat's REST API or a self-hosted verifier on Supabase Pro.

### Offline trust through a signed token with a deadline

RevenueCat's cache alone cannot answer "how long should a phone trust PRO without a connection". It serves cached CustomerInfo offline indefinitely and gives no staleness signal ([RevenueCat docs: Caching](https://www.revenuecat.com/docs/test-and-launch/debugging/caching); [community #3635](https://community.revenuecat.com/sdks-51/how-do-we-differentiate-if-customerinfo-is-missing-entitlements-because-not-purchased-or-because-offline-3635)). ABOT should keep its own record: `get_my_entitlement()` returns a token `{sub, tier, src, exp_store, iat, valid_until, kid}` signed with an Ed25519 key held in Supabase secrets, and the public key ships in the app bundle with `kid` allowing rotation. For subscriptions, `valid_until` is the store expiry plus seven days, matching Play's default grace period, during which Google itself keeps access open before account hold removes it ([Android subscription lifecycle](https://developer.android.com/google/play/billing/lifecycle/subscriptions)). For lifetime, a rolling 90-day window that refreshes silently on any connection is a reasonable balance: a refunded lifetime survives at most 90 offline days, and an honest buyer who trains offline for three months sees a single "connect to verify" prompt, never a data loss. Signing stops a user from editing IndexedDB, but not from winding the clock back; licensing vendors such as Keygen say clock tampering is outside what a signature can fix and recommend heuristics plus periodic online checks ([Keygen docs](https://keygen.sh/docs/api/security/)). A stored high-water mark of the latest time seen, with PRO frozen if the clock falls more than two hours behind it, is enough for a Rp 29.000 product. On Android, Play's local `queryPurchasesAsync` cache is a second offline signal, the same mechanism RevenueCat's offline entitlements rely on ([RevenueCat blog](https://www.revenuecat.com/blog/engineering/introducing-offline-entitlements/)).

Anti-tamper work should stop there. Play Integrity is free up to 10,000 requests a day ([Android: Play Integrity](https://developer.android.com/google/play/integrity/overview)) but does not stop a patched APK from flipping a local flag. The only gates that cannot be bypassed are server-side ones, such as RLS on cloud backup tables, which is a reason to consider whether cloud sync belongs in PRO.

### Restore, refunds and revocation follow the account

Because every purchase carries the Supabase uid, restore becomes "sign in and fetch the token". On Android, a restore button should also call `queryPurchasesAsync` and let RevenueCat bind any Play purchase to the signed-in uid. RevenueCat's default restore setting transfers purchases to the restoring id unless they contain an active subscription, and **one-time purchases always transfer** ([RevenueCat docs: Restore behavior](https://www.revenuecat.com/docs/projects/restore-behavior)), so a Play lifetime purchase could hop between accounts; the Edge Function should log re-binding and flag repeats. Refunds and chargebacks arrive as `SUBSCRIPTION_REVOKED`, `voidedPurchaseNotification` and `ONE_TIME_PRODUCT_CANCELED`, with the Voided Purchases API as a pull backstop, and a newer `pendingRefundReviewNotification` gives the developer **24 hours** to contest a chargeback ([Android RTDN reference](https://developer.android.com/google/play/billing/rtdn-reference)). Each sets `revoked_at` on the matching `entitlements` row, and the next token the device fetches reflects it. Web refunds go through the Midtrans dashboard and the same webhook. The entitlement logic must read subscription *state*, granting access in Active, Cancelled-before-expiry and Grace and denying it in On hold, Paused and Expired ([Android: Integrate](https://developer.android.com/google/play/billing/integrate)), not just compare an expiry date.

### The CLAUDE.md rule needs a written exception

CLAUDE.md says "Zero External Paid API: no runtime network calls, no tokens", with optional Supabase sync as the only documented exception. Every option in this report breaks that rule. Play Billing calls Google at purchase time, RevenueCat ships a public SDK key and becomes a paid service above US$2,500 MTR, Midtrans needs a server key in Edge Function secrets, and the signed-token refresh is a periodic network call. The owner should approve a scoped exception before implementation, and it should state that network calls happen only at purchase, restore, and opportunistic entitlement refresh; that every workout, log and chart works with no connection; that no user data leaves the device for billing beyond the Supabase uid; and that the only paid dependency is RevenueCat above its free threshold. CLAUDE.md and `src/lib/entitlements.ts` (whose header reads "no server, no network") should be updated in the same change.

## Keep the log free, sell the coach, and default to annual

The draft prices sit in the right place. Hevy charges **US$2.99/month, US$23.99/year and US$74.99 lifetime** ([Hevy pricing](https://hevy.com/pricing)), and Strong US$4.99/month or US$29.99/year ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/app/id464254577)). Adapty puts Indonesia at a **0.7 pricing index** against the US ([Adapty 2026](https://adapty.io/state-of-in-app-subscriptions/)), which makes a regionally adjusted Hevy about Rp 34.500 monthly, Rp 277.000 annual and Rp 866.000 lifetime at the assumed exchange rate. ABOT's monthly and lifetime are close to that, and its annual is noticeably cheaper. Local anchors agree: Spotify Student costs **Rp 29.900** and Individual Rp 59.900 ([Kontan](https://lifestyle.kontan.co.id/news/cek-daftar-harga-berlangganan-paket-spotify-premium-pasca-kenaikan-agustus-2025)), Apple Fitness+ costs Rp 69.000 monthly or **Rp 399.000 a year** in Indonesia ([detikInet](https://inet.detik.com/mobile-apps/d-8256746/apa-itu-apple-fitness-dan-harga-langganan-di-indonesia)), and a local gym logger, BodyLog, asks Rp 50.000 a month ([Jagoan Hosting](https://jagoanhosting.app/product/bodylog-pencatat-latihan-gym)). ABOT's monthly therefore reads as "a student Spotify", and its annual as half of Apple's. No confirmed Google Play Indonesia prices for Hevy, Strong or Fitbod were found; someone with an Indonesian account should check the store directly.

Annual should be the pre-selected plan. It drives **59% of Health and Fitness subscription revenue** ([RevenueCat SOSA 2026](https://www.revenuecat.com/state-of-subscription-apps)), and ABOT's 43% annual discount sits inside the 33-50% range of Hevy, Strong and Fitbod. Annual and lifetime also suit Indonesian payment behaviour: wallet balance and carrier billing handle one-off payments more easily than renewals, and the visible *patungan* account-sharing market ([JoinBareng](https://joinbareng.com/blog/layanan-bisa-dipatungan-2026)) points to resistance to recurring charges. There is room to test Rp 249.000-279.000 annual once conversion data exists.

The lifetime price follows common guidance and the founders price needs a cap. At 4.0x annual, Rp 798.000 is inside the 3-4x anchor that makes a subscription look cheap ([AppOpportunity](https://appopportunity.com/blog/how-to-price-indie-app)), against Hevy's 3.1x and Liftosaur's 2.5x. The risk is that lifetime captures "your most loyal users... the ones most likely to keep renewing without one" ([Airbridge](https://www.airbridge.io/en/blog/should-you-offer-a-lifetime-subscription)). Founders at Rp 399.000 is 2.0x annual, below every competitor, so it must be capped by seat count and a closing date, sold mainly on the web where it nets 30% more, and tracked as a share of all purchases. On Play, the simplest implementation is a separate limited one-time product that is removed when the cap fills. No quantitative study of founder-offer cannibalisation was found, so the cap size is a judgement call.

Trials should be seven days on annual. Trials of 17-32 days convert at a median **42.5%** against 25.5% for trials of four days or less, and 55.4% of 3-day-trial cancellations happen on day zero ([RevenueCat blog](https://www.revenuecat.com/blog/growth/7-day-trial-subscription-app)). RevenueCat's own 7-versus-14-day test found 14 days produced slightly more starts but fewer conversions, so it reverted to seven. A 14-day trial is still worth testing for ABOT because a lifter needs three or four logged sessions before the overload coach shows anything. Whether Indonesian users on prepaid carrier billing can start a Play trial at all was not confirmed. Health and Fitness trials convert at a **39.9% median** ([Athletech News, citing RevenueCat 2025](https://athletechnews.com/fitness-apps-monetizable-winner-take-all-or-most/)), the best of any category, but no Indonesia-specific conversion data is public.

The free tier should keep logging, history and data, and gate intelligence and volume. Hard paywalls convert downloads to paid at **10.7% against 2.1%** for freemium by day 35, yet year-one retention is nearly identical at 27% versus 28% ([RevenueCat SOSA 2026](https://www.revenuecat.com/state-of-subscription-apps)), and hard paywalls carry higher refund rates, 5.8% against 3.4% ([ARPU Brothers](https://arpubrothers.com/blog/2025-saas-mobile-apps-trends/)). For an offline logger whose free log is the hook, the right reading is a freemium core with a prominent, dismissible onboarding paywall and contextual paywalls at PRO features. Competitors confirm the line: Strong caps free users at 3 routines with unlimited workouts ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/app/id464254577)), Hevy at 4 routines, 3 months of history and 7 custom exercises ([Hevy Help](https://help.hevyapp.com/hc/en-us/articles/35119778922263-Hevy-Pro-Subscription-How-to-get-Pro-and-What-Does-It-Include)), and Hevy's history cap is the most criticised friction in reviews ([Gainframe](https://gainframe.app/blog/hevy-app-review/)). ABOT's current `ProFeatureKey` list (overload coach, symmetry radar, recovery inspector, voice logger, smart swap, story presets and watermark, unlimited routines, Health Connect autosync, body phase protocols) matches what competitors gate. Two items in the code need reconciling: the paywall matrix in `src/lib/entitlements.ts` lists "Workout logging, routines, records" and "Cloud backup and Health Connect sync" as free, while `unlimited_routines` and `health_connect_autosync` are PRO keys. The cleanest resolution is free logging with 3-4 routines, full history, PRs, basic charts and export forever, never locking existing data after a downgrade, with routine count, Health Connect autosync and the analysis features in PRO.

## Four phases and the decisions that unlock them

The rollout should move money onto the web first, where ABOT keeps the most and Google's policies do not apply, then bring Play Billing live once the Play account is through closed testing. The current `FOUNDERS_BETA` and `COMMERCIAL_LIVE` build modes map cleanly onto this sequence.

| 1. | 2. ## Conclusion

The research shifts ABOT's monetization question from "which payment provider" to "which channel carries which product". Play is the right home for auto-renewing monthly and annual plans because it already speaks GoPay, OVO, DANA and pulsa and handles renewals and VAT. The web is the right home for lifetime and founders sales because a non-PKP individual seller keeps about 30% more per sale there. Treating these as two complementary channels, joined only by a Supabase account, avoids both UCB's engineering cost and Play's anti-steering risk, and matches what an Indonesian buyer who prefers one-off wallet payments is likely to do anyway.

The less obvious implication is architectural. ABOT's offline-first promise survives monetization only if "offline" is redefined from "never touches a network" to "never needs one to work". A signed token with a deadline does that, and it also fixes a latent bug in the current design, where account entitlements can only grow and refunds can never reach the device. Making that change, and writing down the owner's exception to the no-network rule, is the real first deliverable. Prices, trials and gating are already close to right and can be tuned once real Indonesian conversion data exists, which no public benchmark currently provides.
